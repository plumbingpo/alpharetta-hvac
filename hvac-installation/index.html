A complete HVAC installation is really three jobs wearing one name: choosing equipment that matches your home, making the duct system able to deliver what that equipment produces, and commissioning the result so it performs on the meter and not just on paper. The industry's habitual shortcut is skipping straight to the equipment swap, bolting a new condenser and furnace or air handler onto whatever came before. Alpharetta HVAC starts where the physics starts: a load calculation built from your Alpharetta, GA home's square footage, insulation, windows, orientation, and air leakage, because an oversized system short-cycles and dehumidifies poorly while an undersized one never catches up.

Once the load is known, we select equipment to fit it, and we pick it as a matched set. Manufacturers rate the outdoor unit, indoor coil, and blower together, so a mismatched pairing quietly gives up the SEER2 and AFUE figures printed on the sticker. You will get a plain-language explanation of what single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed equipment actually do, where an ECM blower earns its cost, and whether a furnace, air handler, or heat pump makes the most sense for your house, with no pressure attached. The ducts get the same scrutiny. Our experienced technicians measure static pressure, track down leaky or undersized runs, and fix the chokepoints while everything is open, since new equipment on bad ducts never reaches its rating.

Installation day runs on discipline: line sets pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed by superheat and subcooling, gas connections leak-tested, venting run to manufacturer spec, condensate trapped and protected by a float switch, and the thermostat configured for the equipment's actual staging. We finish with full commissioning, recording temperature split, temperature rise, amp draws, and static pressure, then walk you through everything before we haul the old equipment away. Why Professional HVAC Installation Matters

An HVAC system is the largest machine in most homes, and installation is where its entire service life gets decided. The failure modes are invisible at handoff: a vacuum never verified means moisture breeding acid in the refrigerant loop, a charge set by feel means efficiency lost for good, mismatched coil-and-condenser pairings mean the rated numbers were never achievable, and duct restrictions mean the blower spends fifteen years straining against static pressure it was never designed for. Refrigerant work is federally regulated and gas and venting mistakes are safety problems, not just performance problems.

Professional installation is a chain of verifications. Our experienced technicians size from a load calculation, install matched combinations, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, weigh the charge and prove it with superheat and subcooling, leak-test the gas side, verify draft, and set blower speeds to hold the rated temperature rise. Then we commission with recorded numbers, which serve as the baseline every future service visit can be checked against. That paper trail of measurements is the difference between a system that delivers its rated efficiency for fifteen years and one that merely runs, and it is standard on every installation we do.

How to Prevent HVAC Installation Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Hold onto your commissioning readings; they make every future diagnosis faster and cheaper
  • Schedule two tune-ups a year: cooling in spring, heating in fall
  • Give the outdoor unit clear space on every side and a gentle rinse each season
  • Deal with minor rattles and comfort changes before they grow into repairs
  • Flush the drain line before summer to keep algae from clogging it
  • Keep supply and return vents open to preserve the airflow balance set at commissioning
  • Change filters on schedule from day one; airflow protects both the coil and the exchanger
