Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Alpharetta, GA

Home / Blog / Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Alpharetta, GA

As the peak of summer approach here in Alpharetta, GA, our air conditioners become essential appliances of our home comfort. These units operate constantly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners have a lifespan and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a significant issue or breakdown might be on the horizon. Recognizing these warning signs early can save you from a surprise system failure on the hottest day of the year, and potentially minimize damage in the long run. Here at Alpharetta HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: Feeling Less Cool Than Before?

One of the most noticeable and irritating signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a noticeable decrease in its cooling capability. You might find yourself adjusting the thermostat lower and lower only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Some areas of your home might be significantly hotter. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. There are multiple potential causes for this problem. It could be a depleted refrigerant charge, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. It's the critical component for heat exchange. Another common culprit is a dirty air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. If the evaporator coils are fouled, cooling suffers, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. Even a failing compressor, the heart of your AC, could be the cause. If you're experiencing poor temperature control, it's time to have a professional from Alpharetta HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: When Your AC Sounds Off

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing new and alarming sounds coming from your unit, it's a definite sign that something is amiss. Worn motor bearings are often the source of grinding or squealing sounds. These bearings, key to rotational movement, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Clicking sounds, especially persistent ones, could indicate a failing relay or a problem with the electrical components. Loud banging or clunking is especially worrisome, as they might suggest a loose part hitting another component. Don't delay in addressing these auditory warnings. Timely attention to these sounds can avert a minor problem from escalating into an expensive fix.

Increased Energy Bills: The Hidden Cost of a Failing AC

Your electric bill mysteriously climbing is a common indicator of AC troubles. If your bills are increasing without a change in thermostat settings, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. Various problems can contribute to this energy drain. As mentioned, a clogged air filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. Leaky ducts can allow cooled air to escape into unconditioned spaces like your attic or crawl space, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. An aging or improperly sized unit may simply be past its prime. Refrigerant leaks also force the system to work harder. Over time, components like the compressor and fan motors can lose their efficiency. If your utility costs are climbing for no apparent reason, it's worth having Alpharetta HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. Our team can help you determine the most cost-effective solution.

Poor Airflow: Is Air Barely Coming Out of the Vents?

Good airflow is crucial for effective cooling and even temperature distribution throughout your home. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have a mere whisper of air than others, it's a strong indication of an underlying problem. Several issues can contribute to poor airflow. A primary offender is a filter choked with dust and debris, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Obstructed return vents can impede the system's ability to pull air in. Problems with the fan motor, separate from the filter, can also cause weak air delivery. A struggling fan motor won't adequately circulate air through your home's ducts. Debris or even physical damage within the ductwork can also restrict air movement. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. Fixing poor airflow is vital not only for your comfort but also to prevent overworking your AC and reducing its longevity.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Unwanted Wetness Near Your System?

Some condensation is expected from an AC, but significant moisture or standing water around the unit is problematic. This is a definite warning sign that something is wrong. Usually, water leaks are due to an obstructed condensate line. The AC dehumidifies, sending collected water through a condensate line. Algae, dirt, or debris can block this line, causing water to overflow the pan. This can result in damage to your property and even pose electrical risks. Leaks can also originate from a cracked or rusted drain pan. If there's a major refrigerant leak, ice can build up on the coils, melt, and cause overflow. If you notice any unusual water accumulation around your AC unit, it's important to call for service right away to prevent further damage. Alpharetta HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: When Your AC Can't Stay On

Your air conditioner should typically run in relatively consistent cycles, turning on to cool your home and then shutting off once the desired temperature is reached. If you notice your AC unit turning on and off much more frequently than usual, this is known as "short cycling" and it's a significant warning sign. Short cycling means your AC isn't completing a full cooling cycle, which is inefficient and puts undue stress on the system. Multiple factors can contribute to short cycling. An oversized unit might short cycle because it cools the air too rapidly, then stops before it can remove humidity effectively. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. A failing compressor or an electrical issue can also lead to short cycling. Whatever the reason, frequent short cycling accelerates wear on AC components, resulting in earlier breakdowns and higher energy use. If your AC is short cycling, it's not operating efficiently or effectively, and it needs professional attention.

Old Age: Considering the Age of Your System

Even if your air conditioner isn't exhibiting any dramatic symptoms, its age is a crucial factor in predicting its eventual failure. Typically, central AC units last 10-15 years, with exceptional cases reaching 20 with good maintenance. An AC unit nearing or past its expected lifespan will be more prone to issues and less efficient. Older units may rely on R-22 refrigerant, which is phased out and costly to maintain due to scarcity. Components in older systems are naturally more worn from years of use. While regular maintenance can certainly extend the life of your AC, there comes a point where repairs become more frequent and costly than investing in a new, more efficient system. When your AC is over 10 years old and exhibiting other warning signs, it's time to think about replacement. Investing in a new, energy-efficient AC can lead to considerable savings and improved comfort for the long term.
